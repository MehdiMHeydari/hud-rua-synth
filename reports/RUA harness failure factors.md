# Fix the harness before trusting success rates

The harness can move a robot-use agent's success rate by more than a model generation does, and it does so for reasons unrelated to how hard the task is. The largest measured effects come from four places. First, **whether the agent gets usable scene geometry**: harness changes alone took GPT-6 Astra on RoboDojo from 29.7 to 50.3, and a pointing tool took Mythos Preview from 6% to 32% on LIBERO ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16009); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-plays-robotics)). Second, **the level of the action interface**: high-level tools took the same models from 18% to 53% ([URAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39018)). Third, **how motion and feedback are reported**: capping each move and reporting the remaining error took Precision tasks from 9.4 to 25.0. Fourth, **reasoning effort**: Opus 5.5 went from 9% to 33% on LIBERO-10 between low and high effort ([LIBERO-10](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30051)). Smaller but consistent effects come from frame conventions, image history and grasp feedback. Our own data shows the same pattern in miniature. Every one of gpt-6-luna's 8 failures on `lift_cube` had a harness cause as its main cause: an undisclosed camera offset, a continuous-gripper trap, an Euler convention trap, or a grasp-line bug. So luna's 2/10 measures our harness, not the task. The fix is a cheap, ordered validation protocol run before calibration. Its new first step is a $0 "feedback truth-table" test that would have caught three of our four failure classes before any model ran. One caveat applies to everything below: nearly all 2026 evidence comes from unreviewed preprints, often single-run with small samples, so treat effect sizes as direction plus rough magnitude, not as constants.

## How much to trust the evidence

Only a few sources are peer-reviewed: EmbodiedBench (ICML 2025), BLINK (ECCV 2024) and RoboSpatial (CVPR 2025). They use 2024-era models. Anthropic's robotics post is a primary lab source but not reviewed, and its headline aid result rests on a 10-task subset. Every 2026 RUA paper cited here is an arXiv preprint: JustRUA, K1, PyRUA-Lean, VIA, URAI, RoboARCade and the LIBERO-10 frontier study. JustRUA, the most useful of them, runs each ablation once on 111 episodes. Its own bootstrap SD is 2.6 points on the full set ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16009)), so its gaps of about 4 points sit near noise. Its behaviour counts (collisions, empty grasps) are the sturdier signal. K1's memory ablation uses 18 cases per setting, so one case is worth 5.6 points ([K1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29389)).

**"The Bitter Lesson Comes to Robotics" (arXiv 2610.25287) is unverified.** Two research notes cite it with detailed numbers, such as 32-64% of grasps closing on nothing and a 40-action budget. A third researcher searched for it and could not find the paper or an abstract. Every number from it below is marked *(unverified)* and none carries a recommendation by itself.

## Observation: geometry matters more than camera count

**Geometry is the largest observation lever.** In JustRUA, simulator depth raised the score from 39.8 to 46.0. Depth predicted from RGB alone reached 42.4, even though its absolute error was about 74 cm, because the ordering of depths was still right. Both cut empty grasps from 41 to about 16. Giving the camera calibration plus code execution let the model triangulate pixels into 3D, and the score reached 50.3 ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16009)). How the information is presented matters as much as having it. Depth shown as a colour image was neutral in Anthropic's manipulation tests, and a robot dog ignored it entirely. Grasp affordances drawn onto the image lowered JustRUA's score to 30.9 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-plays-robotics)). The point for us is not "add depth". It is that **whether geometry is provided defines what the benchmark measures**, so it must be a fixed, recorded choice.

**More cameras are not reliably better.** Adding a third-person camera changed scores by -3.6, +5.8 and +10.7 points across three Claude models ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-plays-robotics)). A second view hurt GPT-4o in EmbodiedBench ([EmbodiedBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09560)). VLMs are weak at matching points across views and at judging relative depth: they trail specialist models by 35-63 points on BLINK ([BLINK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12390v1)). **Frame mismatch is a measured cost.** When JustRUA switched from absolute world targets to relative offsets, the arm hit objects 20 times instead of 7. The authors' explanation is that each offset "asks for one more subtraction before each move" ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16009)). An undisclosed camera-to-finger offset is the same kind of hidden transform. No paper measures it directly, but it was our most common failure.

**Images after every move are needed. Image history and resolution have optima that depend on the setup.** Returning images only when the agent asks for them dropped success from 83.0% to 68.5% ([PyRUA-Lean](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01939)). On history the evidence conflicts. Keeping every image beat keeping the last two (34.7 vs 29.7) in JustRUA's plain harness. In K1's tool-rich harness, success dropped once more than two past image rounds were included (61-67% vs 78-83%), because stale positions competed with the current view ([K1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29389)). The difference is that K1 has retrieval tools that stand in for memory. A plain `move_to` harness like ours is closer to JustRUA. EmbodiedBench found 500x500 images beat both 300x300 and 700x700 for 2024 models ([EmbodiedBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09560)). No 2026 resolution ablation exists.

## Action interface: conventions fail silently

**Absolute world-frame end-effector targets are the evidence-backed default.** This is the reverse of policy-learning intuition, where delta actions usually win. Relative targets cost JustRUA 14 points on Precision tasks and tripled collisions ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16009)). There is no LLM evidence for joint-space control. It would require the model to compute forward kinematics in its head. **Abstraction level is a difficulty knob, not a free gain.** URAI's high-level tools took four models from 18% to 53% ([URAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39018)). JustRUA's semantic operations changed behaviour but not the score (34.6 vs 38.0), and a buggy high-level tool in URAI needed 7 revisions. Higher abstraction moves capability from the model into the harness.

**Orientation and gripper conventions are where harnesses set traps.** Both JustRUA and URAI avoid general 3D rotation: orientation is measured from a fixed straight-down grasp, or only yaw is exposed. Fine-tuning a 7B VLM on Euler angles instead of an axis representation dropped one task from 96.7% to 42.0% ([ReasonManip](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12744)). That result is indirect for frontier models. HUD DirectControl uses intrinsic-XYZ Euler angles bounded to [-π, π]. With our straight-down pose at `(π, 0, 0)`, three problems follow by derivation; none has been measured yet. Roll sits on the ±π wrap. Positive yaw turns clockwise as seen from the top camera. And roll = 0 is a valid-looking command that points the gripper up. Gripper polarity also varies across harnesses: 0/1, -1/+1, closed-is-0 or closed-is-1 ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16009)).

**Silent motion failures look like model failures.** In the LIBERO-10 study, arms achieved only 64-72% of the commanded displacement on average. Claude models blamed "didn't reach the target" in 88-91% of their failure notes but could not diagnose why ([LIBERO-10](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30051)). JustRUA traced a hidden planner bug that swept the arm across the table. Capping each move at 20 steps and reporting the remaining error took Precision from 9.4 to 25.0 ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16009)). DirectControl rejects targets outside its bounding box. An IK-unreachable target inside the box, though, plays out until the arm stalls, with only a non-zero `off:` residual to show for it. Later moves also start from the last *commanded* target, not the measured pose. That means a stalled target can keep pulling the arm toward an unreachable dimension the model never named again. This "sticky phantom target" is our reading of the code and still needs checking in our env.

## Feedback, memory and prompting: smaller levers, still larger than noise

**Reasoning effort is the largest prompting-side lever, and it does not move in one direction.** On LIBERO-10, Opus 5.5 scored 9%, 18% and 33% at low, medium and high effort. Opus 5 scored 10%, 12% and 6%. GPT-6 Luna scored 0% at every level ([LIBERO-10](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30051)). Astra peaked at medium effort in JustRUA. Anthropic found extra reasoning hurt manipulation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-plays-robotics)). These effort swings are larger than most task-difficulty effects, so "provider default" is not a controlled setting. **One in-context demo is the biggest prompt lever** (53.5% to 73.0% in [RoboARCade](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08209)). It also leaks the solution, so demos must stay out of the measured condition.

**Grasp feedback helps only when it is correct.** Compact readings beat verbose JSON (39.8 vs 38.0). The JSON version also pushed 38% of episodes into context compaction ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.16009)). Interpreted grasp lines ("fingers met; nothing between them") remove a reading-comprehension failure, but they have blind spots, for example with thin objects. A model trusts a wrong label more readily than a raw number. *(Unverified: Bitter Lesson reports weaker models regrasping bowls they already held because a thin pinch read like an empty one.)*

**How episodes end shapes the measured number.** In LIBERO-10, give_up accounted for 681 of 1,400 failed episodes, and false done for 287 ([LIBERO-10](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30051)). Models claimed success on 24-39% of attempted subtasks that were not done ([RoboARCade](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08209)). The success signal should stay hidden from the model, and the checker should end the episode the moment success occurs. Dropping the first turn from context made models forget conventions and loop ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-plays-robotics)). At 30 steps, keeping the full history is affordable and safe.

## Consolidated checklist

| Factor | Recommended default | Evidence | Risk if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Views | Fixed set for all tasks (top-down + wrist); ablate a new view once per model | Third-person view: -3.6 to +10.7 by model ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-plays-robotics)); 2nd view hurt GPT-4o ([EmbodiedBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09560)) | Ranking reflects view preference |
| Frames and camera offsets | One world frame; state image-to-world axes; mount the wrist cam on the finger axis or state the offset | Relative frame: collisions 20 vs 7 ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16009)); offset itself unmeasured | Constant miss read as "hard task" |
| Images per move | Automatic after every move | On-demand: 83.0% → 68.5% ([PyRUA-Lean](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01939)) | Agent acts blind |
| Image history | Full at ≤30 steps for a plain harness; fixed across runs | Full 34.7 vs last-2 29.7 (JustRUA); >2 rounds hurt with tools (K1) | Forgets occluded state, or confuses stale state |
| Resolution | 256² for cost; one-off check at ~512² | 500² best of 300/500/700 ([EmbodiedBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09560)) | Small-object tasks fail on pixels |
| Geometry (depth, calibration, poses) | A declared choice; use privileged versions only as a diagnostic arm | Empty grasps 41 → 16; score to 50.3 with calibration + code (JustRUA); cursor 6% → 32% (Anthropic) | Difficulty silently becomes "metric depth from RGB" |
| Overlays | None by default | Boxes +8-12 pts (EmbodiedBench); crosshair misread, drawn affordances hurt (Anthropic, JustRUA) | The overlay becomes a failure source |
| Target type | Absolute world-frame `move_to` | Relative: Precision 25.0 → 10.9 (JustRUA) | Arithmetic errors counted as skill |
| Orientation | Expose x, y, z, yaw, gripper unless the task needs tilt; make straight-down `(0,0,0)` | Both leading harnesses anchor to straight-down; Euler fine-tune 96.7 → 42.0 ([ReasonManip](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12744)) | Wrap, sign-flip and upside-down stalls |
| Gripper command | Binary open/close, or snap to 0/1; command on a different scale from the state reading | JustRUA and EmbodiedBench use 0/1 or binary; our echo drop | Agent "holds" by echoing a reading and drops |
| Per-move travel | Cap each move; report steps run and the remaining error | Precision 9.4 → 25.0 (JustRUA) | One bad move ends the episode |
| Unreachable / stall | Explicit reason ("unreachable", "blocked"); reset the commanded target to the measured pose after a stall | Achieved 64-72% of commanded; Claude blames non-reach in 88-91% of failure notes ([LIBERO-10](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30051)) | Silent stalls and phantom targets |
| Abstraction | `move_to` only, no `grasp(obj)` | URAI 18% → 53%; JustRUA semantic ops no gain | Hides the measured capability |
| Grasp feedback | Interpreted line that matches the sim in every state, plus the raw width | Compact 38.0 → 39.8 (JustRUA) | Wrong label trusted over the image |
| Readings verbosity | Compact text | JSON pushed 38% of episodes into compaction (JustRUA) | Context pressure looks like horizon difficulty |
| Success signal / done | Hidden; the checker auto-ends; log done-without-success | 24-39% false claims (RoboARCade) | Grader leakage or hidden miscalibration |
| give_up | Keep; log the step index and remaining budget | 681/1,400 failures (LIBERO-10) | Quitting threshold looks like difficulty |
| System prompt | Fixed, task-agnostic, states every convention; never drop turn 1 | First-turn drop → loops (Anthropic) | Convention errors in the first moves |
| Demos | None in the measured condition | 53.5% → 73.0% (RoboARCade) | Measures imitation |
| Reasoning effort | Pin explicitly per model and record it | Opus 5.5 9/18/33%, Opus 5 10/12/6% (LIBERO-10) | Effort swing exceeds task effect |
| Step cap | ≥3x the oracle's step count; report the cap-hit rate | Budget-hit episodes 0 → 3 successes with more moves (JustRUA) | Budget exhaustion read as difficulty |

## Validate the harness in eight cheap steps

Run the steps in order. Each one rules out one explanation for a low success rate, and only a number that survives all of them counts as task difficulty. The costs are our estimates for MuJoCo on CPU.

1. **Feedback truth-table test ($0, scripted).** Drive the sim through every state the text feedback can describe and assert that each line matches the sim. Cover gripper openings from 0 to 1, with and without a close command. Check that each orientation the prompt describes as "down" actually points down. Project the fingertip point into the wrist image and check where it lands. Command an unreachable target and check what the feedback says, and check what the next partial `move_to` does after a stall. This is the step the literature lacks. Three of our four failure classes would have failed it.
2. **Scripted oracle plus a negative oracle ($0).** The oracle must pass through the real tool. A "looks done but isn't" run, such as lifting the cube and then dropping it, must fail the checker. This rules out broken physics and a success check that can be gamed ([ABC](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825v5)).
3. **Prompt-vs-scorer diff (5 min).** Every condition the checker tests must appear in the prompt, with a tolerance the observations can resolve. JustRUA found 5 failing tasks where the prompt or the scoring was at fault ([JustRUA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16009)).
4. **Human through the same interface (~15 min, 3-5 attempts).** Mehdi drives `move_to` using only the rendered images and feedback text. Below about 80% success, fix the observation channel. No RUA paper does this. Computer-use benchmarks do ([OSWorld](https://os-world.github.io)).
5. **Cheap-model pilot plus failure labels (10 rollouts).** Label each failure with a fixed taxonomy: empty grasp, drop, collision, placement miss, stop-short, false done, give_up, harness fault, prompt/scorer mismatch. Confirm every "harness fault" label by replaying the agent's exact commands in the sim. LLM judges are only about 70-89% precise ([AgentRewardBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08942)). **Gate: if more than about 1 in 5 failures is a harness fault, fix it and repeat from step 1.**
6. **Privileged-perception ablation (10 rollouts on the cheap model).** Add ground-truth poses or sim depth. A large jump, such as 2/10 to 6/10 or more, means the task mostly measures seeing through our cameras. Then either record it as a perception task or change the cameras.
7. **Two-model discrimination on shared seeds.** The stronger model should score clearly higher. If both are at 1/10 or below while the oracle passes, suspect the harness.
8. **Calibrate with the harness pinned, then re-audit.** Record the harness version, views, effort and cap with every number. Use n ≥ 20 on fixed seeds, since 2/10 has a 95% interval of roughly 3-56%. Re-audit tasks in the 5-30% band periodically. In a 2026 audit of SWE-bench Verified, 59.4% of the items o3 could not reliably solve turned out to be flawed ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)).

## Applying it to our Panda harness: luna's 2/10 is a harness number

Every one of luna's 8 failures has a harness cause as its main cause. The 4 wrist-offset episodes fall under frames and camera offsets. The camera sits about 7 cm off the finger axis, yet our env notes say the wrist image "looks straight down the fingers". That sentence is actively wrong, so centring the cube in the wrist view produces a systematic miss.

The gripper-echo drop falls under gripper command. The state reports 0.62 while the fingers hold the 5 cm cube, and the command uses the same 0-1 scale. Echoing 0.62 back therefore asks for exactly the current width with no squeeze, and the cube falls. Both roll = 0 stalls fall under orientation and stall reporting. Down is `(π, 0, 0)`, so roll = 0 aims the gripper up, and the arm stalls with nothing but an `off:` residual.

The grasp-line episode falls under grasp feedback. In `grasp_sentence`, any opening above 0.75 returns "open" before the close command is checked, so a 0.81 partial close is labelled "open". The claimed slip that did not reproduce in the sim is the one genuinely model-side error: a false belief about state. It belongs in the profile.

None of this means luna would succeed with these fixed. A model that walks into these traps may fail later for other reasons. It does mean 2/10 cannot be used as a difficulty number.

**These traps also distort discrimination, not just levels.** Sol's 8/10 against luna's 2/10 looks like healthy discrimination, but part of that gap is how well each model resists our quirks. Sol's 2 failures need the same labelling. The view result (shoulder-only 0/10, shoulder + wrist 0/10, top-down + wrist 2/10) cannot separate the conditions at n = 10. LIBERO-10's 0% for Luna at every effort level ([LIBERO-10](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30051)) suggests luna is also genuinely weak here, which makes it a poor calibration model.

For the rest of the current setup, the checklist gives these concrete changes:

| Setting | Change |
|---|---|
| Wrist camera | Mount it on the finger axis (preferred, since nothing is left for the model to reason about), or state the 7 cm offset and its direction in the notes |
| Gripper | Make it binary, or snap commands below 0.5 to 0 and above to 1; state that the reading is width, not a command |
| Orientation | Restrict dims to x, y, z, yaw, gripper for lift and pick tasks, or redefine the tool frame so straight down is `(0, 0, 0)`; add an "unreachable / blocked" stall reason; reset the commanded target after a stall |
| Grasp line | Branch on the close command before the "open" threshold; add the step-1 truth-table test |
| Notes | State the reach tolerance (about 1 cm, 0.05 rad) and the yaw direction as seen from the top camera |
| Resolution (256²) | Keep it, and run one 512² check |
| Step cap (30) | Fine for `lift_cube`; set it per task family at 3x or more of the oracle's steps and log cap hits |
| Reasoning effort | Replace "default" with an explicit, recorded value per model |
| History | Keep it full and record it |

Then re-run the luna and sol pilots (step 5) before any calibration.

## Conclusion

The literature measures harness effects mostly as *missing capability*: giving geometry, tools or effort raises the score. Our data points to a second class that the literature barely measures, which is *active traps*. These are conventions and feedback lines that are wrong or misleading, and they push scores below what the model could do with an honest but plain interface. Traps are cheaper to find than capability gaps, because a scripted truth-table test finds them without any model calls. They are also more dangerous, because they reward models that happen to resist them, and that inflates apparent discrimination.

The practical shift for the pipeline is to treat the harness as a versioned artifact with its own test suite, validated once per change rather than per task. Task calibration should be reported only against a pinned harness version. Once the traps are gone, the remaining failures are the capability profile we actually want: false beliefs about state like luna's phantom slip, depth judgement, correction efficiency, and honest stopping.
